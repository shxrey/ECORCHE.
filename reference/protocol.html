Execution Standard

The S-Tier Execution Protocol

Apply this 3-1-X tempo to every working set across all 6 sessions. It isn't decoration — each phase targets a distinct, evidence-backed driver of hypertrophy, whatever muscle is on the bar that day.

3s

Controlled Negative

Yield to the load on the way back to the stretch — never let the weight free-fall. A slow, resisted eccentric keeps the target muscle under mechanical tension longer per rep, the mechanism Schoenfeld's review identifies as a primary driver of hypertrophic signaling.

Schoenfeld (2010), J Strength Cond Res 24(10)
1s

Deep Stretch Hold

Dead-stop at the point of maximum muscle lengthening before reversing — full stretch on a press, full flexion on a curl, full hip hinge on an RDL. A growing body of muscle-length research finds training performed at longer lengths produces significantly greater hypertrophy than the same exercise stopped short — the basis for pairing every movement in this program with a genuine end-range pause.

Sport Sciences for Health (2025); Maeo et al. (2021, 2022); Pedrosa et al. (2022); Kassiano et al. (2023)
X

Explosive Concentric

Drive back with maximum intentional velocity while keeping strict form. Intent, not just load, recruits the highest-threshold motor units — the difference between a rep that merely moves weight and one that actually signals growth.

Intent-based loading principle
Programming Standards

Rest, Progression & Deload

The tempo and RIR targets tell you how to perform a set. These four rules tell you how to string sessions together over months without stalling or breaking down. Weekly order: Legs A → Push A → Pull A → Legs B → Push B → Pull B → 1 full rest day. If a second rest day is needed, take it after Pull A rather than skipping a session.

⏱

Rest Periods

Heavy compounds and singles (Back Squat, Flat/Incline Bench, RDLs, rows): 2–3 min. Standard isolation (curls, extensions, raises, pushdowns): 60–90 sec. The lat pulldown pre-exhaust superset and the calf pairing: 15–20 sec between A→B, then a full 90 sec after the pair before moving on.

🔥

Warm-Up

5 minutes of light cardio, then 1–2 ramping sets (roughly 50%→75%→90% of your top working weight) before the first compound of each day. Isolation and machine movements only need a single light warm-up set — save the ramping for whatever's heaviest and most technical.

📈

Double Progression

Work within the given rep range on every exercise. Hit the top of the range on every working set at your target RIR → add weight next session (small jumps: +2.5–5% for compounds, the smallest available plate/pin for isolation). Drop below the bottom of the range at the same weight → hold the load and rebuild reps first. For FAILURE/PEAK sets, the target is simply beating last session's rep count at the same weight. Log weight × reps × RIR every session — without a written log, RIR targets quietly drift within 2–3 weeks.

🔄

Deload — Every 6th Week

Cut every exercise to roughly 60% of normal volume (drop 1 set per exercise, 2 sets max), keep everything at 3+ RIR, and skip every FAILURE/PEAK set entirely for the week. Non-negotiable at this volume and frequency — it's what lets the program run indefinitely without the joints or CNS quietly breaking down underneath it.

Effort Calibration

Reading The Pressure Gauge

Every set in this program is prescribed by Reps in Reserve (RIR) — how many more reps you could have done before your form broke down. This is the needle you're aiming for, on every one of the 6 days.

Needle resting on 1–2 RIR — where most working sets in this program live.

0 RIRFailure
1 RIR1 Rep Left
2 RIR2 Reps Left
3+ RIRToo Easy

Training recommendation: the majority of sets across all 6 sessions sit at 1–2 RIR. A meta-analysis of 11 controlled trials found no significant difference in hypertrophy between sets taken to complete failure versus stopping 1–2 reps short, provided effort was genuinely high — meaning you don't need to grind every set to zero to grow. This program reserves true 0-RIR failure for a small number of strategic moments per session — single heavy compounds, drop-set burnouts and small-muscle finishers — where the extra fatigue cost is cheapest.

Helms, Cronin, Storey & Zourdos (2016), Strength & Cond J 38(4); Grgic, Schoenfeld, Orazem & Sabol (2022), J Sport Health Sci 11(2)
Volume Audit

Weekly Set Volume, Muscle By Muscle

The evidence-based sweet spot for hypertrophy sits at roughly 10–20 hard sets per muscle per week, with smaller, faster-recovering muscles often needing less and large multi-region muscles like the back sometimes benefiting from a bit more. Here's every muscle in this program counted straight from the exercise cards above — not estimated. Updated for the new 6-day split: triceps split into two standalone lifts, core/shrugs/forearms isolated onto Push/Pull/Legs respectively, and Hack Squat + Leg Curls swapped for Back Squat + a 4th RDL set.

Optimal

🧮 How these numbers were counted

Every set is credited to every muscle its exercise card explicitly tags as a target — a Back Squat's 3 sets count toward both Quads and Glutes, because it's a genuine primary driver of both, not just a stabilizer. Unilateral work (the plate-loaded row) is counted once per side, matching how much stimulus each side of the muscle actually receives. Nothing here is padded with loose "indirect" credit beyond what the program's own activation data already shows.

The verdict: splitting the triceps superset into two standalone lifts and isolating core/shrugs/forearms onto their own days didn't change any muscle's total weekly volume — it just cleaned up the session structure. The one number that actually moved is hamstrings, down to 8 sets/wk now that Leg Curls are gone in favor of a 4th RDL set; that's the one spot in the new split worth keeping an eye on. Front delts remain the other muscle running hotter than the rest, and lateral delts, hamstrings & calves are the three spots with legitimate headroom if any of them is a priority for you.

Research Sources

Peer-reviewed studies and practitioner breakdowns that inform the exercise selection, tempo, and intensity prescriptions across all 6 sessions — the same literature Jeff Nippard and Dr. Mike Israetel draw on for their own evidence-based programming.

Core science, applied to every day: these four sources underpin the universal tempo, RIR, and volume rules used across Push, Pull and Legs alike.

01

Schoenfeld, B.J. (2010). The Mechanisms of Muscle Hypertrophy and Their Application to Resistance Training.

The foundational review defining mechanical tension, metabolic stress, and muscle damage as the three drivers of hypertrophy. Forms the theoretical basis for the tempo protocol used on every day of this program.

02

Helms, E.R., Cronin, J., Storey, A., & Zourdos, M.C. (2016). Application of the Repetitions in Reserve-Based Rating of Perceived Exertion Scale for Resistance Training.

Introduced the RIR-based effort scale used throughout every session's intensity prescriptions, letting load autoregulate to daily readiness instead of chasing a fixed number on the bar.

View on PubMed →
03

Grgic, J., Schoenfeld, B.J., Orazem, J., & Sabol, F. (2022). Effects of Resistance Training Performed to Repetition Failure or Non-Failure on Muscular Strength and Hypertrophy.

A meta-analysis of 11 studies concluding training to failure isn't required for hypertrophy — the basis for prescribing 1-2 RIR on most sets across all 6 days and reserving true failure for a handful of strategic moments per session.

View on PubMed →
04

Schoenfeld, B.J., Ogborn, D., & Krieger, J.W. (2017). Dose-Response Relationship Between Weekly Resistance Training Volume and Increases in Muscle Mass.

The foundational meta-analysis establishing a graded, roughly linear relationship between weekly hard sets and hypertrophy — the basis for the weekly volume targets referenced throughout the Anatomy section.

View DOI →
01

Trebs, A.A., Brandenburg, J.P., & Pitney, W.A. (2010). An Electromyography Analysis of 3 Muscles Surrounding the Shoulder Joint During a Chest Press at Several Angles.

Compared pectoralis and deltoid activation across bench angles from 0° to 56°. Found the clavicular head peaks at a steep ~44° incline — the basis for the incline vs. flat exercise pairing in Push Day A/B.

Find on Scholar →
02

Rodríguez-Ridao, D. et al. (2020). Effect of Five Bench Inclinations on the EMG Activity of the Pectoralis Major, Anterior Deltoid & Triceps Brachii During the Bench Press.

Tested 0°, 15°, 30°, 45° and 60°. Upper pec peaked near 30°, mid/lower pec peaked at 0° flat, and anterior deltoid activation rose steadily with steeper angles — directly informing the Day A/B angle split.

View on PMC →
03

Quick Reference

Rest Periods

Heavy compounds & singles: 2–3 min. Standard isolation: 60–90 sec. Superset pairs: 15–20 sec between A→B, then 90 sec after the pair.

RIR (Reps in Reserve)

0-1 RIR = pushing right up to the wall. 1-2 RIR = one clean rep left in the tank. FAILURE = absolute, 0 RIR, technical form breaks next rep.

Double Progression

Hit the top of the rep range at your target RIR on every set → add weight next session. Fall below the bottom of the range → hold the load and rebuild reps first.

Deload — Every 6th Week

Cut volume ~60% (drop 1-2 sets/exercise), stay 3+ RIR, skip all FAILURE/PEAK sets.
